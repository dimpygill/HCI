Welcome!

Every day, we interact with websites, mobile apps, computers, kiosks, smart devices, and other digital systems. Some are easy and intuitive to use. Others leave us wondering: “Why did they design it this way?”

In this course, we will explore that question from a computer science perspective.

What is Human-Computer Interaction?

Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and other digital systems—and how we can design those interactions to be more effective, efficient, accessible, and easy to use.

As computer science students, you have spent considerable time learning how to make software work correctly. In this course, we will add another question:

Can people actually use the software effectively?

A program can be technically correct and still provide a poor experience for its users. We will study how people perceive information, remember things, make decisions, perform tasks, make mistakes, and respond to interface design.

What will you learn?

You will explore these ideas and apply them to an interactive system:

  • HCI and usability
  • Human perception, attention, memory, and cognitive load
  • User needs and user-centered design
  • Interaction design principles
  • Graphical User Interface (GUI) design
  • Visual hierarchy, layout, typography, and color
  • Wireframes and prototypes
  • Accessibility and inclusive design
  • GUI implementation and event-driven interaction
  • Usability evaluation
  • User testing and usability data
  • Emerging interaction, including AI and conversational interfaces

Our design process

Throughout the course, we will follow a human-centered design process:

  1. Understand the problem
  2. Understand the users
  3. Identify tasks and requirements
  4. Design
  5. Prototype
  6. Implement
  7. Test with users
  8. Evaluate
  9. Improve the design

Design is iterative. We rarely create the perfect interface on the first attempt. We design, evaluate, learn from users, and improve—returning to earlier steps as needed.

Your HCI Design Project

You will identify a real-world problem involving human interaction with technology and progressively develop a solution. Your project will grow in stages as you learn about users, design, prototyping, accessibility, implementation, and usability evaluation.

Possible project ideas include:

Campus & learning

  • Course registration
  • Student advising
  • Campus navigation
  • Library system

Everyday tasks

  • Healthcare appointment scheduling
  • Restaurant ordering
  • Personal finance or fitness
  • Event registration or task management

Understand the user and the problem before designing the solution. Your design decisions will become more informed as you gather evidence and evaluate your work.

A different way of thinking about software

When developing software, we often ask, “Does the program work?” In HCI, we also ask:

Understanding & efficiency

Can users understand the interface, find what they need, and complete their tasks efficiently?

Mistakes & feedback

Does the system help prevent mistakes and provide useful feedback when something goes wrong?

Accessibility

Can people with different abilities use the system?

Evidence

How do we know whether our design supports users effectively?
